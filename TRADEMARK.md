# Trademark policy

Veduta's **code** is free software: AGPL-3.0-or-later for the core, Apache-2.0 for the SDK,
schemas, plugins and examples. See [LICENSING.md](LICENSING.md). Those licences give you broad
rights over the code, and nothing here narrows them.

The **name** "Veduta" is different. A licence to copy and modify software is not a licence to call
the result by the original's name, and this short policy exists so that the boundary is written
down rather than assumed.

## What you may do without asking

- Say that your project, product, article, video or service **works with**, **supports**,
  **integrates with**, or **is built on** Veduta.
- Use the name to refer to this project accurately — in documentation, comparisons, reviews,
  conference talks, package descriptions, or a list of things you run.
- Redistribute Veduta unmodified, under its own name, with its licences and notices intact.
- Publish an integration or plugin and name it descriptively — for example "Veduta integration for
  Foo", or a package named `veduta-foo`.

## What needs a different name

- A **modified version** distributed to others. Fork freely — the licence guarantees that — but
  give your fork its own name. Users need to be able to tell whose software they are running and
  who to report a bug to.
- Anything that would suggest this project **endorses, sponsors, maintains or vouches for** your
  work when it does not. That includes company, product and domain names built around "Veduta",
  and using the name as the primary identity of a hosted service.

The test is not whether the name appears, but whether a reasonable person would be misled about
the origin of the software or about who stands behind it.
